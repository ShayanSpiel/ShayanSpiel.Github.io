Buyer stage

Planning or buying

Your team has identified a workflow that could become an agent. Engineers or vendors are waiting for a specification. Before anyone writes code, the system needs to be defined, bounded, and tested on paper.

Main symptoms

You know these signs

The workflow lives in someone's head. Nobody agrees what the agent should and should not do. The team is choosing a framework before defining the problem. Stakeholders expect a demo before a design.

Inputs required

What I need from you

Workflow examples, exception cases, current process documentation if it exists, access to the people who do the work, and honest answers about what success looks like.

What is not included

Boundaries

No production code. No framework selection. No deployment. No ongoing development or maintenance. The deliverable is an architecture and validation package your engineers or a vendor can build from.

{% include components/section-head.html tag='Process' title='Seven steps from workflow to decision' sub='' %}
Step 1

Collect real workflow examples and exceptions

I gather actual cases, edge conditions, failure scenarios, and decision logs to ground the architecture in operational reality instead of abstractions.

Step 2

Define the current human workflow

The existing process is mapped end to end. Every decision point, handoff, escalation, and exception becomes visible before any agent boundary is drawn.

Step 3

Decide what the agent may and may not do

Authority boundaries are set. The agent receives clear permission rules: where it decides, where it recommends, and where a human must approve or act.

Step 4

Design roles, state, tools, context, memory, and approval points

The full agent system is specified: how roles divide responsibility, how state persists, what tools exist and how they are governed, what enters context, how memory works, and where humans interrupt.

Step 5

Derive evals from real workflow evidence

Evaluation scenarios come from the actual cases collected in step one. Every eval traces to a real workflow, not a synthetic ideal.

Step 6

Define acceptance thresholds

Pass and fail criteria are calibrated against operational requirements. The team knows before building begins what good enough looks like.

Step 7

Produce a recommendation

Build, revise, buy, or stop. The decision is supported by the evidence gathered and the architecture designed.
