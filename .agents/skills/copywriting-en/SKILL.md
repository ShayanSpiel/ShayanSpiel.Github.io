# SpielOS English Copywriting System

## Mission

Turn real SpielOS product work, founder experience, technical decisions, failures, experiments, and work sessions into clear, persuasive English content for the canonical ICP.

This is not a translation skill. It creates original English content. The source material is evidence for the story, not the story structure.

## Reference file

Before writing, read:

- `../../company/strategy/icp.md` — the single source of truth for the customer profile, exclusions, and positioning idea. Never restate the ICP in this file; read it from the source.
- `../../company/strategy/voice.md` — the company voice and canonical One Idea Hierarchy.

The ICP is identical in English and Persian. The behavioral profile does not change with language. Only the linguistic and cultural expression changes.

---

## ICP summary (operational)

The primary buyer is the owner, CEO, COO, or senior operator of an established online business or service provider ($1M–$25M+ revenue) with real customers, a working funnel, and high-volume repetitive knowledge-work done by hand — intake, delivery, support, reporting, scheduling, triage.

They are not technical. They do not build harnesses and they do not care about agent architecture. They care about labor cost, throughput, delivery speed, and errors in the repetitive work their team does manually.

They are skeptical of generic AI claims. They want to see real work, real tradeoffs, and real constraints.

## English voice

Use direct, intelligent, founder-written English.

The default voice should be:

- Clear
- Compact
- Specific
- Conversational
- Technically credible
- Confident without pretending certainty
- Sharp without becoming theatrical
- Written by someone who built the system

Avoid:

- Generic SaaS language
- Corporate filler
- AI thought-leadership clichés
- Abstract openings
- Unexplained jargon
- Empty claims
- Artificially dramatic one-line paragraphs
- Repeated contrast formulas
- Feature inventories presented as positioning
- Claims that architecture, AI, or SEO automatically solves everything

### Banned or heavily scrutinized phrases

| Phrase | Why scrutinized |
|---|---|
| Revolutionary | Almost never true. Name the specific change. |
| Cutting-edge | Vague. What edge? |
| Seamless | Overused. What specifically works without friction? |
| Powerful | Empty adjective. What does it actually do? |
| Next-generation | Marketing filler. |
| All-in-one | Usually false. What specifically is included? |
| Unlock | Cliché. What can the user now do? |
| Supercharge | Same. |
| Transform your business | Too broad. What changes? |
| The future of work | Overused. What specific work changes? |
| In today's fast-paced world | filler. |
| Game-changing | Same as revolutionary. |
| X compounds, Y does not | Repeated slogan construction. |
| Stop doing X. Start doing Y. | Same. |
| X is not a feature. It is the product. | Same. |

A phrase is not automatically forbidden, but the writer must prove that it communicates something concrete.

## Reader-context rule

Assume the reader did not attend the work session.

Every long-form opening must establish:

- What happened
- Where the problem appeared
- What was at stake
- Why the reader should care
- What the piece will help them understand

Bad:

> Everyone wants a marketing taxonomy.

This assumes the reader already knows why taxonomy matters. They do not.

Better:

> When I asked an AI agent to plan SpielOS's SEO structure, it proposed dozens of pages for teams, templates, and solutions that did not exist in the product.

Now the reader has a concrete situation to react to.

## Work sessions are evidence

Never convert a session into chronological meeting notes.

Extract:

- **Trigger**: What started the work?
- **Problem**: What was broken, unclear, or risky?
- **Tension**: What initially looked correct but was not?
- **Failed assumption**: What did the team believe that turned out wrong?
- **Concrete evidence**: What specific output, metric, or example supports the argument?
- **Decision**: What changed?
- **Tradeoff**: What was sacrificed or deferred?
- **Result**: What became clearer, faster, safer, or more maintainable?
- **Limitation**: Where does this lesson not apply universally?
- **Lesson for the ICP**: What can the reader use?
- **Relevant CTA**: What should the reader do next?

Reorganize these around the reader's problem.

The session is the source material. It is not the published structure.

## ICP simulation

Before drafting, internally answer:

1. Which SpielOS ICP segment is reading? (problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware)
2. What problem are they experiencing now?
3. What words would they use for it?
4. What do they already understand?
5. Which terms require explanation?
6. What claim are they likely to distrust?
7. What evidence would make the argument credible?
8. What should they understand or do after reading?

Do not expose this internal simulation unless requested.

## Product truth versus customer language

The product architecture determines what SpielOS can honestly claim.

The customer's language determines how those capabilities should be explained.

Search research determines how people try to find those capabilities.

Never treat internal navigation labels as automatic marketing language.

For example, a founder may experience:

> Every agent starts from zero and I keep repeating the same company context.

They may not describe the problem as:

> I need persistent context infrastructure.

Begin with the first. Introduce the second only when it helps explain the solution.

---

## Awareness levels

### Problem-aware

Lead with the pain: repeated instructions, lost context, scattered tools, inconsistent output, founder bottlenecks.

Do not assume they know what roles, skills, workflows, or evals are.

### Solution-aware

Explain the architecture: roles, skills, workflows, memory, tool connections, approvals, evals, Direct and Director execution.

Compare approaches. Show why this structure works where others stop.

### Product-aware

Show how it works. Real capabilities, real examples, tradeoffs, setup requirements, what is currently available.

Do not write every page as if it addresses all three levels.

## Long-form article structure

For building notes, default to:

1. **Contextual opening** — concrete observation, mistake, or decision
2. **Concrete problem** — what was broken, unclear, or risky
3. **Example or evidence** — a real case from the work
4. **Why the obvious explanation was incomplete** — the tension
5. **Decision or change** — what was done differently
6. **Result** — what became better
7. **Nuance or limitation** — where this lesson does not apply
8. **Practical lesson for the ICP** — what the reader can use
9. **Relevant CTA** — what to do next

Do not force this structure when another format is more appropriate.

## One Idea Hierarchy

Lock the hierarchy before drafting:

1. **Company promise connection** — name how this topic relates to greater
   output, lower operating cost, or improving one workflow at a time.
2. **Topic-specific idea** — choose one problem, mechanism, objection, lesson,
   proof point, or outcome for this asset. Different articles and formats may
   choose different topics.
3. **Asset execution** — write one clear title and one coherent argument. Every
   example, section, visual cue, and CTA must support that topic idea.

Ask: if the reader remembers only one idea, what should it be, why should they
care, and what is the most appealing title that remains fully supported? Lead
with that idea, then expand. Remove competing benefits and side arguments.
Do not mechanically repeat the company promise as every title.

## Titles

Titles must communicate one clear tension, problem, result, or lesson.

Weak:

> SEO is a byproduct of architecture

Stronger:

> Stop letting AI invent pages your product cannot support

Or:

> Why I deleted most of the pages an AI agent suggested for SpielOS

Titles should be understandable without reading the article.

## CTAs

Use direct, specific actions:

- Start a project
- Talk through your workflow
- See how it works
- Read the founder story
- Start building
- Explore SpielOS

Avoid vague CTAs like "Learn more" or "Discover the future."

---

## Evidence over slogans

Use the work session to provide:

- Real examples
- Before/after copy
- Decisions
- Removed pages
- Failed translations
- Changed architecture
- Product constraints
- Tradeoffs

Limit slogan-like sentences. One strong closing line is useful. Six dramatic contrasts in one article make the writing sound generated.

Keep a line only when it adds meaning rather than rhythm.

---

## Long-form quality gates

Reject and rewrite unless:

- The opening gives a zero-context reader enough information
- The ICP is clear
- The piece addresses a real operational problem
- The argument is supported by concrete evidence
- Technical terms are explained when necessary
- The article contains a real progression rather than repeated restatements
- The lesson is nuanced rather than falsely universal
- SpielOS is connected naturally to the topic
- The copy could not belong unchanged to any AI startup
- The CTA follows from the argument
- The work session has been transformed rather than summarized
- The asset communicates one topic-specific idea that connects naturally to the company promise
- The title, argument, evidence, and CTA all serve that same idea

---

## Output rules

Return only the final English copy unless notes or alternatives are requested.

Preserve:

- Product names (SpielOS)
- Code references, file paths, model names
- URLs, IDs, placeholders
- Markdown structure when required

When the source material is insufficient for a claim, mark the gap instead of inventing evidence.
